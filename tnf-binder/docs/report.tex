\begin{abstract}
We designed candidate binders to human TNF-$\alpha$ required to satisfy three objectives at
once: bind human TNF-$\alpha$, cross-react with the mouse ortholog, and bind at pH~7.4 while
releasing at pH~6.0. Four generative pipelines proved substantially interchangeable; the
outcome was determined almost entirely by \emph{verification}. Of 57 constructs entering the
gate stack, 3 were submitted and 2 passed the organiser's novelty check --- a yield of
$2/57 \approx 3.5\%$. The highest-scoring construct by every interface
metric was verbatim human ubiquitin. One design was submitted and failed the organiser's
automatic novelty check, establishing empirically that \emph{fold} similarity, not sequence
novelty, is the binding constraint. We report the full funnel including failures, and state
explicitly what remains unmeasured: there is no measured binding of any kind.
\end{abstract}

\section{Target and epitope}

The epitope was computed rather than taken from literature. TNF-$\alpha$ residues within
$4.5\unit{\AA}$ of TNFR2 in PDB~\texttt{3ALQ} give 33 contact positions. Intersecting these
with a human/mouse alignment of the soluble domain (124/157 $=$ 79.0\% identity, reproducing
the organiser's stated figure) partitions them into 24 conserved and 9 divergent positions:
P20H, R31Q, S71D, T72--, H73Y, V85I, T89E, I97V, A111L.

\paragraph{R31$\rightarrow$Q is the trap.} It carries the largest solvent-accessible surface
area in the entire epitope ($193\unit{\AA^2}$) and sits adjacent to R32 in the 29--33 loop
engaged by adalimumab and infliximab. This is why neither therapeutic is mouse cross-reactive.
Any design gripping that loop satisfies Objective~1 and forfeits Objective~2.

Two conserved sub-patches were considered: a lobe (S86, Y87, Q88, K90, V91, N92) at 90\%
conservation, and a pocket (113, 115, 143, 144, 146, 147) at 100\%. A screen found no
significant difference between them (Table~\ref{tab:levers}).

\section{The verifier stack}

Each verifier below caught something that had already passed every check preceding it.

\subsection{Composition}
A model-free check on amino-acid usage. It eliminated 50 of 57 constructs. The necessity is
established by a single observation: a sequence that is 59\% alanine received a
binder-to-target ipTM of $0.556$ and ranked first in a 40-design shortlist. Interface
confidence models score degenerate sequences confidently.

\subsection{Sequence novelty}
Exact 20-mer matching against PDB~\texttt{seqres} (1.17M chains) and Swiss-Prot (576k).
A genuine de novo sequence essentially never shares a 20-mer with a natural protein, making
this decisive rather than statistical. It rejected the single highest-scoring construct of the
campaign --- best interface score, lowest interface PDE ($0.89\unit{\AA}$), an
ideal-pKa histidine --- which was \textbf{verbatim human ubiquitin}.

\subsection{Interface}
All interface quantities derive from per-residue buried surface area (unbound minus bound
SASA). Contact-pair counting was implemented first and was actively misleading: counts scale
with interpenetration, so clashing poly-alanine designs out-scored real ones on every ratio
built from them.

\subsection{Fold novelty}
The organiser scores novelty 1--4 and requires $\geq 3$; level 4 demands sequence identity
$\leq 30\%$ \emph{and} less than moderate structural similarity, where similarity is TM-score
weighted by coverage. We implemented this with FoldSeek and TM-align over CATH50.

\paragraph{Empirical calibration.} One submitted design \textbf{failed} the organiser's
automatic check and two passed. The three differed by $0.6$ percentage points in sequence
identity (37.5\% vs.\ 38.1\%), so sequence novelty cannot explain the split. The failing design
matched CATH~2.60.40 --- the immunoglobulin-like $\beta$-sandwich --- at TM-score $0.893$ over
88.8\% coverage. Fold similarity decided it.

Our gate predicts the failure correctly but \emph{under-scores} both passes, returning 2 where
the organiser returned $\geq 3$. It is therefore a conservative pre-filter: decisive on
failure, advisory on borderline. An earlier claim that it reproduced all three verdicts was
incorrect, arising from a missing sequence-identity value defaulting to zero.

\subsection{pH switch}
Objective~3 requires a histidine whose p$K_a$ lies \emph{between} the two assay pH values:
neutral at 7.4 so the binder binds, cationic at 6.0 so it releases. Three mechanisms were
derived from first principles and each was refuted by PROPKA predictions on real designs
(Table~\ref{tab:pka}).

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering
\caption{Proposed p$K_a$ mechanisms against measurement ($n=50$ substitutions).}
\label{tab:pka}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
Hypothesis & Prediction & Measured \\
\midrule
Buried His pays desolvation penalty & raises effective switch & $r=-0.37$; buried sites at p$K_a$ 3.1--3.6 \\
His adjacent to cation is repelled & lowers p$K_a$ usefully & $r=+0.11$ (wrong sign) \\
Carboxylate proximity raises p$K_a$ & raises into window & $r=+0.21$; net $+6 \rightarrow$ p$K_a$ 3.6 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

No structural descriptor predicted switchability. Positions therefore come from an exhaustive
per-position scan: 200 substitutions yielded 18 ideal (p$K_a$ 6.3--7.2) and 17 usable
(6.0--7.6). Multi-histidine combinations were verified to retain their p$K_a$ within
$0.21$ units of the single-mutant values, supporting a cooperative switch.

\section{Cross-model disagreement}

No single confidence score is treated as truth, because the models disagree on identical
structures (Table~\ref{tab:models}).

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering
\caption{Binder-to-target interface confidence by predictor.}
\label{tab:models}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
Structure & AlphaFold2 & Boltz-2 & BoltzGen folding \\
\midrule
BindCraft (AF2-optimised) design & $\mathbf{0.92}$ & $\mathbf{0.21}$ & --- \\
BoltzGen designs & --- & 0.44--0.64 & $\sim 0.20$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

AlphaFold2 rating its own optimised designs at $0.92$ where an independent predictor returns
$0.21$ is self-confirmation, not validation. Two further artefacts were identified and
corrected: \emph{global} ipTM is unusable on a multi-chain target (the TNF trimer's internal
interfaces score $\sim 0.88$ and dominate the mean, so a dimer$\rightarrow$trimer change lifted
global ipTM $0.20 \rightarrow 0.52$ while the binder interface stayed flat), and best-of-$n$
reporting inflates (metrics are stored per diffusion sample; we rank on the mean).

Measured across all 57 folded constructs, global ipTM overstates the designed interface by
$+0.405$ on average --- roughly half its own value. Every interface number in this report is
instead a \texttt{pair\_chains\_iptm} entry between the binder chain and each target chain,
symmetrised over both matrix directions (which differ by up to $0.08$), averaged across target
chains, then averaged across diffusion samples. Best-of-$n$ and mean differ by up to $0.35$
within a single construct.

\subsection{Confidence was not sufficiency}

Six constructs cleared the $\sim 0.50$ threshold conventionally taken to indicate a credible
de novo interface. \emph{None was submittable}, and the reasons were not subtle
(Table~\ref{tab:toprank}).

The second and third rows are the instructive ones: real sequences, genuine interface
confidence, and an interface placed somewhere other than the conserved patch they were
conditioned on. Epitope focus --- not confidence --- was the operative constraint, and a gate
reading confidence alone would have passed all six. This is also why the remaining generator is
BindCraft2 with forced targeting and hard coldspot filters rather than further BoltzGen
sampling.

\section{Reproducibility}

The gate stack is installed and run as one command-line tool; each subcommand prints the
measurement a claim in this report rests on.

\begin{verbatim}
tnf-binder epitope                     # the computed epitope and its divergent positions
tnf-binder confidence <predictions/>   # rank designs on mean binder-to-target ipTM
tnf-binder verify <complex.cif>        # composition / interface / pH verdicts for one design
tnf-binder validate <submission/>      # submission format pre-flight
tnf-binder wandb-log <assets/>         # funnel, tables and structures to Weights & Biases
\end{verbatim}

The campaign run, with all 57 constructs' confidence tables, the p$K_a$ scans and the folded
complexes as interactive 3D media, is at
\href{https://wandb.ai/chaleong/tnf-conditional-binder/runs/545bu3v1}{wandb.ai/chaleong/tnf-conditional-binder}.
A report built from that run's own panels --- every table and every folded complex embedded
rather than described --- is
\href{https://wandb.ai/chaleong/tnf-conditional-binder/reports/Campaign-Report--VmlldzoxODA5MDQ5OQ==}{here},
and an interactive walkthrough of the architecture is published as a
\href{https://claude.ai/artifact/UWyFVrYW8cx6DXftKf1F8z}{Claude Artifact}.

\section{What is not established}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{No measured binding of any kind.} No expression, size-exclusion chromatography,
thermal stability, or SPR/BLI. Every number here is computational.
\item pH selectivity rests on predicted p$K_a$. No folding model represents protonation state,
and the assay is not simulated.
\item Mouse cross-reactivity rests on a \emph{predicted} mouse structure and conserved-epitope
coverage, not on measured binding to mouse TNF-$\alpha$.
\item Interface confidence is model-dependent and unresolved; we report a range.
\item No validated mechanism for the pH switch replaces the three that were refuted. Positions
are empirical.
\end{itemize}

A generated sequence that has passed computational checks is a candidate. A result is a measured
biological endpoint on a preserved funnel, with controls. This work produced the former.
