The pipeline as it runs

The current best (0.465, data-fork) is the 0.456 PubChemLite recipe plus four changes from dmitriigluzdov's version 26: GLACIER proxies kept where ICEBERG scores exist, joint ICEBERG+GLACIER re-ordering of proxy-covered lists, five fold-safe FPNets in the PubChem channel plus a sixth PubChem slot at rank 24, and a candidate-aware rank-1 PubChem promotion (Saner Turhaner, 0.465 upstream). The 0.456 recipe is the E006 pipeline drawn below with three changes from its public lineage: PubChemLite structures added to engine 2's library (0.451 → 0.455), GLACIER proxy scores from other adducts for molecules without an [M+H]+ spectrum (N1A), and the PubChem popularity weight at 0.15 instead of 0.25 (0.456). Its 0.451 base is the E006 recipe with promotion at rel > 150, the rank-2 hedge and standardised ChEBI/LIPID MAPS candidates. Stage counts are not visible: the leaderboard scores a hidden test, and a commit run processes only 12 smoke molecules (ICEBERG and GLACIER scored 11 each; 400 output rows). What changed on 24 October

A new best: another public notebook forked unchanged, on top of the 0.456 fork from 20 October. The de novo route was closed offline before spending any submission on it.

  • 0.465: the new best. dmitriigluzdov's public notebook “CASMI 26: The Data Behind Spectra to Structures”, version 26, run unchanged as private kernel casmi-data-fork (vendored at casmi/notebooks/fork464/), scored 0.465 (submission 57588653, 24 October): +0.009 over pubchemlite-fork, above the ±0.005 noise, so G1 passes. Over the 0.456 recipe it keeps GLACIER proxies where ICEBERG scores exist, re-orders proxy-covered lists by ICEBERG and GLACIER jointly, puts five fold-safe FPNets in the PubChem channel plus a sixth PubChem slot at rank 24, and promotes a PubChem candidate to rank 1 with a candidate-aware rule (Saner Turhaner, 0.465 upstream). The gate (gate_fork451.py) passed: 400 rows, 12 smoke molecules full, ICEBERG and GLACIER 11 each.
  • 0.456 reproduced: the previous best. Hongbin Gu's public notebook “casmi26-v465-v15-pop015”, run byte-identical (md5 9d1ab164…081d) as private kernel casmi-pubchemlite-fork (version 1), scored 0.456 (submission 57435777, 20 October), its public score exactly: +0.010 over e006-fork. Its lineage from the 0.451 notebooks: PubChemLite structures in engine 2's library (0.455), N1A GLACIER proxies for molecules without an [M+H]+ spectrum, then popularity weight 0.25 → 0.15 (0.456). The gate passed: ranker 160 features and 4 boosters, 0 errors, ICEBERG and GLACIER 11 smoke molecules each, 400 rows; it attaches 15 public datasets, including a PubChemLite candidate-fingerprint set, on the same GPU image as the other forks.
  • FRIGID de novo: 0 / 50, closed. Offline on an Apple M5, no submission. On 50 Enveda-180 [M+H]+ molecules with no public spectra, given the true formula, FRIGID put the answer in its top 10 for 0 of 50; the bar, fixed before the run, was 5 of 50. The best of each top 10 reached mean Tanimoto 0.230 to the answer; 94% of generations were valid, at 35 s per spectrum. The encoder is the bottleneck: MIST's predicted fingerprints reach Tanimoto 0.26 on these spectra against 0.63 on MassBank, while the decoder fed the true fingerprint recovers 49 of 50 (inflated, since it was probably pretrained on these structures).
  • FPNet cannot replace MIST: closed. On 50 Enveda-180 molecules held out of the fold-safe FPNet, its fingerprints reach Tanimoto 0.245 in its own 10,226-bit space and 0.180 mapped to FRIGID's 4096-bit Morgan, against 0.268 for MIST on the same molecules. It keeps only 1,464 of the 4,096 ECFP4 bits. Retraining a 4096-bit head would take an estimated 10–15 T4 hours; not justified.
  • Next: a daily public-notebook check. Single E006 settings look tuned and the de novo route failed offline, while the public lineage gained +0.044 in a week (0.421 → 0.465). Fork any credible improvement that reproduces. The leaderboard top is 0.504; the weekly GPU quota (30 h) resets 31 October 00:00 UTC (11:00 Sydney).

What changed on 17 October

  • The E006 fork is the new best: 0.446. The public notebook “CASMI26 | E006 + PubChem Promo rel>150 | LB 0.446”, run byte-identical as our private kernel casmi-e006-fork (version 2), scored 0.446 (submission 57298936), reproducing its public score exactly: +0.031 over fork-v1 and +0.090 over v19. Its lineage is flexonafft's v80 recipe (0.436) → E006, which fuses a second engine's ranks and adds ChEBI/LIPID MAPS candidates and simulated ranker rows (0.442) → PubChem promotion at rel > 150 instead of 250 (0.446).
  • One run failed before submission. Kernel version 1 used the source's metadata, which names Kaggle's CPU image; the notebook's T4 preflight stopped it (nvidia-smi exit 12) and it was not submitted. Version 2 runs on the same GPU image as fork-v1. Its commit run took 1183 s: engine ready, ranker 160 features and 4 boosters, 0 base errors, ICEBERG 11 of 12 and GLACIER 11 of 11 smoke molecules scored, 400 output rows. Scoring the hidden test took about 4 hours.
  • The fork is the new best: 0.415. The public notebook “CASMI26 | v4n Fusion + PubChem on public 0.421”, run unchanged as our private kernel on a T4, scored 0.415 (submission 57288940), +0.059 over v19. The commit run took 1286 s, loaded the ranker (160 features, 4 boosters) and built every base list without error.
  • Why our pipeline lost. It excluded the train structure behind each copied spectrum, but the copies are a property of the visible test, which is train examples; the leaderboard scores a hidden test. The fork uses train matches as evidence.

What changed on 13 October

The leaderboard scores a hidden test; the visible test is train examples. Every copy-targeted change was tuned to the wrong data.

  • The visible test is train examples. The 0.421 notebook says so in its code and detects the hidden test by a signature of its spectrum ids. Our "1189 of 1213 test spectra copy a train row" describes the visible test only, which is why excluding the copied structure (+0.001), the similarity filter and its thresholds all measured as noise.
  • Noise measured directly. An identical re-run of v19 scored 0.353 against 0.356, so run-to-run noise is about ±0.003.
  • The 0.421 notebook's edge. Its base ranking searches every spectrum against all of train, propagates matches to similar structures, and ranks a train ∪ COCONUT ∪ generated pool with a public LightGBM ranker (160 features); PubChem enters only when the library match is weak. We exclude what it uses as evidence.
  • Natural-product pool. For 280 general held-out molecules, COCONUT holds 27 answers and our pool already finds all 27. For 265 held-out natural products the popularity shortlist already holds 215 of 247 answers (MRR@25 0.703); COCONUT and the second pass add at most one. The pool is not the gap.

What is not established

  • No local estimate of the score. Train-derived labels were wrong, and public notebooks cannot supply labels: their downloadable outputs predict only about 12 smoke-test molecules and write a placeholder (CCO) for the rest, because full predictions run only inside Kaggle's scoring rerun. An earlier reading of “three lineages agreeing on 12 of 400” was those placeholders. Train cannot supply a test-like held-out set: only 41 isomer pairs in 2.54 million rows share a spectrum, and 7 split across the fold-safe FPNet's training the way the test's copied spectra do.
  • Why fork-v1 scores 0.415 on our account against 0.421 public. Our two runs agree, so the 0.006 is not run-to-run noise, and the E006 fork reproduced its public 0.446 exactly on the same account and day, so it is not a general account or day effect either; a different input version of the 0.421 notebook is possible but unchecked.
  • Why the 0.451 notebook scores 0.445 for us. Its 0.451 is reported for version 52, and Kaggle serves only version 53, which adds ranker seeds; whether those seeds or non-reproduction explain the 0.006 is not established.
  • Whether a de novo route could help with a better encoder. FRIGID found 0 of 50 offline with MIST's fingerprints; an encoder fine-tuned on Enveda's spectra is untested, and the 39–52% of the test outside PubChem and COCONUT is the SOTA sweep's estimate, not a measurement on the hidden test.
  • What in the version-26 notebook earns its +0.009. We ran it unchanged; how the gain splits between the proxy re-ordering, the fold-safe FPNets, the sixth PubChem slot and the candidate-aware promotion is not measured by us.
  • What in the 0.456 notebook earns its +0.010. We ran it unchanged; its author's steps (PubChemLite +0.004, N1A and popularity 0.15 +0.001) are public scores we did not reproduce one by one.
  • Why E006 gains. The 0.446 notebook stacks several changes over the 0.421 lineage (engine-2 fusion, ChEBI/LIPID MAPS candidates, simulated ranker rows, the metric-identity overlays, the rank-2 hedge, promotion at rel > 150); we ran it unchanged, so how much of the +0.031 each contributes is not measured by us. Its author chose rel > 150 on the public leaderboard.
  • The private score. The forks' settings were fixed by their authors before we saw any result, but tuned on the public leaderboard (the E006 notebook reports its threshold sweep there), so 0.446, 0.456 and 0.465 may be optimistic on the private split (the 0.456 notebook chose popularity 0.15 there); our own shortlist size, forward-model weights, GLACIER's input and v19's similarity threshold were chosen on the public split, so 0.356 is optimistic.
  • A per-molecule breakdown. The hidden test is not visible, so which molecules the forks gain on, how many have a library match (similarity ≥ 0.7), and how many get forward-model evidence is unknown.
  • Candidate recall on the hidden test. On held-out train molecules the wider PubChem channel adds answers (255 against 236 of 280), yet fork-v2 scored the same as fork-v1; how much hidden-test recall either buys is unmeasured.
