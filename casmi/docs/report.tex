\title{Molecule identification from MS/MS for CASMI 2026:\\ one change per submission, from 0.115 to 0.465}
\author{Charlene Leong}
\date{24 October 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We rank candidate structures for 400 molecules from their tandem mass spectra in the CASMI 2026
Kaggle competition, scored by MRR@25 on the InChIKey connectivity block. The first submission
scored 0.115 against a local estimate of 0.664; the estimate came from labels drawn from the same
data as the candidates, and was retracted. Since then every number reported is a public
leaderboard score, and each of the 18 submissions changed one thing (a nineteenth re-ran v19 unchanged). A PubChem-scale pool with a
popularity prior, a learned spectrum-to-fingerprint model, and two forward models that predict each
candidate's spectrum take the score to 0.349; a second PubChem pass that avoids structures similar
to the copied train rows takes it to 0.356, against 0.421 for the public notebook these
components come from. Running that notebook unchanged as our own kernel scored 0.415, $+0.059$
over the best of our own pipeline. Running a later public notebook, E006 with PubChem promotion at
$\mathit{rel} > 150$ (public 0.446), unchanged as our kernel reproduced its public score exactly:
0.446, $+0.031$ over the first fork and $+0.090$ over our own best. It was the base every later
change was measured against: raising its engine-2 fusion weight from 0.6 to 0.8 scored 0.437, and a
third public notebook (0.451 upstream at its version 52) forked unchanged at its current version 53
scored 0.445. A fourth public notebook of the same lineage, which adds PubChemLite structures to the
second engine's library (public 0.456), reproduced its score exactly as our kernel: 0.456, $+0.010$
over the E006 fork. A fifth, dmitriigluzdov's version 26, which adds joint ICEBERG+GLACIER re-ordering,
fold-safe FPNets in the PubChem channel and a candidate-aware rank-1 promotion, scored 0.465 as our
kernel, $+0.009$ over the 0.456 fork and the current best. A de novo route was closed offline before any submission:
FRIGID, given the true formula, placed the answer in its top 10 for 0 of 50 held-out molecules against
a pre-registered bar of 5. 26 submissions were made and all were scored. No local estimate of the
score exists.
\end{abstract}

\section{Task}
Each test molecule has one or more LC-MS/MS spectra (400 molecules, 1213 spectra). A submission
lists up to 25 SMILES per molecule; the score is the mean reciprocal rank of the answer, matched on
the first 14 characters of the InChIKey. The competition runs notebooks offline, so every database
and model is attached as a dataset. Only the public split of the leaderboard is visible.

\section{Method}
\textbf{Current best: a fork of the public version-26 notebook (data-fork, 0.465).} The submission is
dmitriigluzdov's public notebook \emph{CASMI 26: The Data Behind Spectra to Structures}, version 26, run
unchanged as the private kernel \texttt{charyeezy/casmi-data-fork}, vendored at
\texttt{casmi/notebooks/fork464/}. It is the 0.456 recipe described next with four changes: GLACIER
proxies kept where ICEBERG scores exist, joint ICEBERG+GLACIER re-ordering of proxy-covered lists, five
fold-safe FPNets in the PubChem channel plus a sixth PubChem slot at rank 24, and a candidate-aware rank-1
PubChem promotion (Saner Turhaner, 0.465 upstream).

\textbf{Previous best: a fork of the public 0.456 notebook (pubchemlite-fork).} The submission was
Hongbin Gu's public notebook \emph{casmi26-v465-v15-pop015}, run byte-identical (md5
\texttt{9d1ab164\ldots}) as the private kernel \texttt{charyeezy/casmi-pubchemlite-fork}, version 1,
on the same GPU image as the other forks, with 15 public datasets attached. It is the E006 pipeline
described next, through the 0.451 recipe (promotion at $\mathit{rel} > 150$, the rank-2 hedge,
standardised ChEBI/LIPID MAPS candidates), with three changes: PubChemLite structures added to
engine~2's library (0.455 upstream), GLACIER proxy scores from other adducts for molecules without an
$[\mathrm{M{+}H}]^+$ spectrum (N1A), and the PubChem popularity weight at 0.15 instead of 0.25 (0.456).

\textbf{Before it: a fork of the public E006 notebook (e006-fork, 0.446).} The submission was the public notebook
\emph{CASMI26 \textbar{} E006 + PubChem Promo rel$>$150 \textbar{} LB 0.446}, run byte-identical (md5
\texttt{9829a966\ldots}) as the private kernel \texttt{charyeezy/casmi-e006-fork}, version 2, on a T4. Its
lineage is flexonafft's v80 recipe (public 0.436), then E006 (0.442), then the PubChem promotion's
structure-evidence clause at $\mathit{rel} > 150$ instead of 250 (0.446). Three candidate sources run
side by side: engine~1 is the v4n pipeline of the previous fork (below, top 60); engine~2 is a separate
two-ranker engine over COCONUT, ChEBI/LIPID MAPS and train structures, with simulated ranker rows (top 40);
and a PubChem-only channel ranks up to 25 non-pool structures by $z(f\cdot z) + 0.25\times$ popularity.
ICEBERG and GLACIER (GLACIER on $[\mathrm{M{+}H}]^+$ only) score the same-formula groups of all three lists.
PubChem structures are merged into the engine-1 list when the library's maximum similarity is below 0.9,
and the first is promoted to rank 1 when $(S > 6 \wedge \mathit{pop} \geq 5) \vee \mathit{rel} > 150$,
where $\mathit{rel}$ is how far the best PubChem $f\cdot z$ exceeds the best pool candidate's. The merged
list is fused with engine~2 by weighted reciprocal rank, $1/(3+r_1) + 0.6/(3+r_2)$, re-ranked by GLACIER
within formula groups, deduplicated by the official metric identity with one vote per identity per
engine, re-ranked by ICEBERG where GLACIER has no scores, and finally engine~2's top structure is
inserted at rank 2 when it differs from rank 1 (\texttt{rank2}).

\textbf{First fork: the 0.421 notebook (fork-v1, 0.415).} From the first fork on 17 October
the submission was the public
notebook \emph{CASMI26 \textbar{} v4n Fusion + PubChem on public 0.421}, run unchanged as the private kernel
\texttt{charyeezy/casmi-v4n-fork} on a T4. It searches every test spectrum against all of train and
propagates matches to analogs; ranks a pool of train $\cup$ COCONUT $\cup$ generated derivatives with a
public LightGBM ranker (\texttt{ranker\_v4b.pkl}, 160 features, 4 boosters); joins PubChem candidates
only when the library's maximum similarity is below 0.7; and re-scores with ICEBERG and GLACIER (off for
library hits), falling back to fragment rescoring where they cannot run. Where our pipeline excluded the
train structure behind each copied spectrum, the fork uses train matches as evidence.

\textbf{Our own pipeline (best 0.356, v19)}, described below, is the record of what we built before the forks.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Candidates.} Within a 5 ppm window of the molecule's neutral mass, the 1000
    best-documented PubChem connectivities (by $\log(1+\text{substances}) + \log(1+\text{PubMed})$)
    from a 106-million-structure tier, plus train structures in the window.
  \item \textbf{Copied spectra.} 1189 of 1213 test spectra are bit-identical to a train row; every
    molecule has at least one. The train structure behind it is removed from the candidates.
  \item \textbf{Ranking.} An ensemble of two public FPNet checkpoints predicts fingerprint logits
    $z$ per spectrum and per polarity-merged spectrum; a candidate with fingerprint $f$ scores
    $f\cdot z$, z-scored within the molecule, plus $0.25 \times$ popularity.
  \item \textbf{Forward models.} For the top 60, ICEBERG and GLACIER predict every candidate's
    spectrum. Within each same-formula group reaching the top 25 the order becomes
    $z(\text{score}) + \lambda\, z(\text{ICEBERG}) + \lambda\, z(\text{GLACIER})$, with $\lambda = 1$.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Results}

re-run; all were scored; none is omitted. One further run, the E006 fork's kernel version 1, failed its
own GPU preflight and was not submitted (below).

Two levers account for almost all of the gain: the learned spectrum model ($+0.115$, v5 to v6)
and the forward models ($+0.087$, v11 to v15). Tuning the shortlist and the prior's weight (v8, v9)
moved the score by under $0.01$, as did removing the copied structures, although that changed 267 top-1 picks:
FPNet, trained on the copied rows, had returned their structures, and what replaced them was as
rarely right. Identical runs differ by about $\pm 0.005$; v16, v17 and v18 fall inside it and were
reverted. v18 is the instructive one: on 280 held-out train molecules, scored by an FPNet that
never saw them, a second PubChem pass chosen by the spectrum lifted the answer's presence among
the candidates from 126 to 236 and MRR@25 from 0.241 to 0.405. On the test set it moved the score
by $+0.004$. The production FPNet was trained on the train rows the test spectra copy, and the
pass's top-1 picks moved toward those copied structures (median Tanimoto 0.26 to 0.42 over the 192
molecules whose top-1 changed). v19 skips second-pass candidates with Tanimoto $\geq 0.5$ to the
copied structure (894 dropped over 400 molecules) and scored 0.356, $+0.007$ over v15. Against v18,
which differs only by the filter, the gain is $+0.003$, inside the noise, so v19 is kept but does
not clear G1; its threshold was also chosen after seeing v18. An identical re-run of v19 scored 0.353, and the
0.4 threshold (v20) 0.354: the filter's effect is not distinguishable from noise.

The fork (fork-v1) scored 0.415, $+0.059$ over v19 and the largest single gain after FPNet. Its commit
run took 1286\,s, loaded the ranker, built every base list without error, injected 600 PubChem rows
in its 12 smoke molecules with no failures, and GLACIER scored 11 of the 12. Widening its PubChem
channel to the top 10{,}000 pass-1 rows (fork-v2), chosen from our recall probe (255 against 236 of 280
held-out answers), scored 0.415 again and was discarded.

The E006 fork scored 0.446 (submission 57298936), its public score exactly, $+0.031$ over fork-v1 and
$+0.090$ over v19. Its first kernel version used the source's metadata, which names Kaggle's CPU image;
the notebook's T4 preflight failed (\texttt{nvidia-smi} exit 12) and it was not submitted. Version 2 runs
on the GPU image fork-v1 uses. Its commit run took 1183\,s: engine ready, ranker 160 features and 4
boosters, 0 base errors, ICEBERG 11 of 12 and GLACIER 11 of 11 smoke molecules scored, the engine-2 cell
applied on 400 rows, and 400 output rows. Scoring the hidden test took about 4\,h. Because this fork
reproduced its public score on the same account and day, fork-v1's 0.006 shortfall is not a general
account or scoring-day effect.

Two later submissions changed or replaced the E006 fork and neither was kept. e006-v3 changed one
byte (cell 35): the engine-2 weight in the final rank fusion, 0.8 instead of 0.6. Its gate passed
(ranker 160 features and 4 boosters, 0 errors, ICEBERG and GLACIER 11 smoke molecules each) and it
scored 0.437 (submission 57310111), $-0.009$, outside the noise. dmitriigluzdov's public notebook
\emph{CASMI 26: From Spectra to Structures} reports 0.451 at its version 52 (E006 with depth-7
engine-2 rankers and standardised ChEBI/LIPID MAPS candidates); Kaggle serves only version 53, which
adds ranker seeds. Forked unchanged as the private kernel \texttt{charyeezy/casmi-spectra-fork} (md5
\texttt{951a34f8\ldots}, the author's 4 datasets, GPU image), it scored 0.445 (submission 57311145),
inside the noise of 0.446.

The fourth fork (pubchemlite-fork) scored 0.456 (submission 57435777, 20 October), its public score
exactly, $+0.010$ over the E006 fork and outside the noise. Its gate passed: ranker 160 features and 4
boosters, 0 errors, ICEBERG and GLACIER 11 smoke molecules each, and 400 output rows; the commit run
took about 1190\,s. Its upstream steps from the 0.451 base (PubChemLite $+0.004$, then N1A and
popularity 0.15 $+0.001$) are its author's public scores; we reproduced only the endpoint.

The fifth fork (data-fork) scored 0.465 (submission 57588653, 24 October), $+0.009$ over pubchemlite-fork
and above the $\pm 0.005$ noise, so it clears G1 and is the new best. Its gate (\texttt{gate\_fork451.py})
passed: 400 output rows, 12 smoke molecules full, ICEBERG and GLACIER 11 each.

\textbf{Two offline checks, no submission.} Both ran on an Apple M5 on 18 October. FRIGID, the
host-approved de novo model (MIST-MSG encoder, FRIGID-base decoder), was run on 50 Enveda-180
$[\mathrm{M{+}H}]^+$ molecules that have no spectra in any public library, given the true formula.
The bar, fixed before the run, was the answer in the top 10 for at least 5 of 50. It found 0 of 50
(top 1, 5 and 10 alike); the best of each top 10 reached mean Tanimoto 0.230 to the answer, 3588 of
3819 generations were valid (94\%, a lower bound), at 35\,s per spectrum. Diagnostics run after the
result locate the failure in the encoder: MIST's predicted fingerprints reach Tanimoto 0.26 to the
truth on these spectra against 0.63 on MassBank, while the decoder fed the true fingerprint recovers
49 of 50 at rank 1, an upper bound inflated because its 230-million-structure pretraining probably
includes these compounds. The follow-up asked whether the fold-safe FPNet could replace MIST. On 50
Enveda-180 molecules held out of its training (re-drawn, since only 10 of the first 50 were held out),
its fingerprints reach Tanimoto 0.245 in its own 10{,}226-bit space and 0.180 mapped to FRIGID's
4096-bit Morgan space, against 0.268 for MIST on the same molecules; it keeps only 1{,}464 of the
4{,}096 ECFP4 bits, and was trained to rank candidates rather than reconstruct bits. Retraining a
4096-bit head would take an estimated 10--15 T4 hours, not justified on this evidence.

\textbf{Closed, with the reason.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item FRIGID de novo with MIST's fingerprints: 0 of 50 in the top 10 with the true formula, against
    a pre-registered bar of 5 of 50; the encoder (Tanimoto 0.26 on Enveda spectra) is the bottleneck.
  \item FRIGID driven by the fold-safe FPNet: Tanimoto 0.180 in FRIGID's bit space (0.245 in its own)
    against MIST's 0.268 on the same 50 held-out molecules; a 4096-bit retrain (10--15 T4 hours) not justified.
  \item Engine-2 fusion weight 0.8 (e006-v3): $-0.009$ against 0.446.
  \item The 0.451 notebook at version 53 (spectra-fork): 0.445, no gain over 0.446.
  \item PubChem promotion at $\mathit{rel} > 100$ on E006: not run. Decided before any submission on
    a reach check from public evidence: its public sibling already scored 0.444 against 0.446.
  \item A different GLACIER checkpoint: ours is ms-pred's public MassSpecGym-trained GLACIER; the
    paper's contrastive fine-tune gives 69.95 against 69.69 MassSpecGym top-1, and its NIST gain needs
    NIST-licensed weights, so there is nothing to swap.
  \item PubChem channel over 10{,}000 rows (fork-v2), forward-model weight 1.5 (v17), GLACIER on
    $[\mathrm{M{+}H}]^+$ only (v16), popularity weight 0.15 (v8): inside the noise or below it.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Next.} The base is now the 0.465 data fork. Single settings of the E006 family look tuned
(promotion threshold swept upstream, engine-2 weight 0.8 lost 0.009, forward models already at 1.0)
and the de novo route failed offline, while the public lineage gained $+0.044$ in a week (0.421 to
0.465). The cheapest lever left is a daily check of new public notebooks, forking any credible
improvement that reproduces. The leaderboard top is 0.504 (24 October). The weekly GPU quota (30\,h)
resets 31 October 00:00 UTC.

\section{What is not established}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Why fork-v1 scores 0.415, not 0.421.} Both our runs of the unchanged notebook score
    0.006 below its public score. The E006 fork reproduced its public 0.446 exactly on the same account
    and day, so a general account or day effect is ruled out; the cause is not established.
  \item \textbf{Why the 0.451 notebook scores 0.445 for us.} Its 0.451 is reported for version 52;
    we could fork only version 53, which adds ranker seeds. Whether the seeds or non-reproduction
    explain the gap is not established.
  \item \textbf{Whether a de novo route could help with a better encoder.} FRIGID found nothing with
    MIST's fingerprints; an encoder fine-tuned on Enveda's spectra is untested. The 39--52\% of the test
    outside PubChem and COCONUT is the SOTA sweep's estimate, not a measurement on the hidden test.
  \item \textbf{What in the version-26 notebook earns its gain.} We ran it unchanged; how its $+0.009$
    over the 0.456 fork divides between the proxy re-ordering, the fold-safe FPNets, the sixth PubChem
    slot and the candidate-aware promotion is not measured by us.
  \item \textbf{What in the 0.456 notebook earns its gain.} We ran it unchanged; how its $+0.010$ over
    the E006 fork divides between PubChemLite, N1A and the popularity weight is its author's public
    scores, not our measurement.
  \item \textbf{What in E006 earns its gain.} We ran the 0.446 notebook unchanged, so how its $+0.031$
    over fork-v1 divides between engine-2 fusion, the ChEBI/LIPID MAPS candidates, the simulated ranker
    rows, the metric-identity overlays, the rank-2 hedge and the promotion threshold is not measured by us.
  \item \textbf{A per-molecule breakdown of the forks' gains.} The hidden test is not visible, so which
    molecules it gains on, and how many have a library match, is unknown.
  \item \textbf{A local estimate of the score.} Train-derived labels rated our first submission at 0.664
    against 0.115 on the leaderboard. Public notebooks cannot supply labels: their downloadable outputs
    score only about 12 smoke-test molecules and write a placeholder (\texttt{CCO}) for the rest, since
    full predictions run only inside Kaggle's scoring rerun; an earlier reading of ``three lineages
    agreeing on 12 of 400'' was those placeholders, as was a local 0.030 for the 0.421 notebook. Nor can train supply a test-like held-out set: only 41 isomer pairs in
    2.54 million rows share a spectrum with at least 3 peaks, and 7 of them split across the
    fold-safe FPNet's training as test answers and copied structures do. Each submission is the only
    measurement.
  \item \textbf{The private score.} Three choices --- the shortlist size, $\lambda$, and GLACIER's
    input --- were selected on the public split, as was v19's similarity threshold, so 0.356 is optimistic.
    The forks' settings were fixed by their authors before we saw any result, but tuned on the public
    leaderboard (the E006 notebook reports its threshold sweep there; the 0.456 notebook chose its
    popularity weight there), so 0.446, 0.456 and 0.465 may be optimistic too.
  \item \textbf{Candidate recall.} Whether the answer is among the candidates cannot be separated
    from ranking with one MRR; a wider PubChem channel added held-out answers but no score (fork-v2).
  \item \textbf{Why the data is shaped as it is.} The copied train structures are the answer for at
    most $\sim$46 of 400 molecules.
  \item \textbf{The environment switch alone.} The move to a pinned Python 3.12 image and RDKit
    2026.3 (v12) was not measured separately.
  \item \textbf{Forward-model coverage on the hidden test.} Our pipeline left 29 visible-test molecules
    without forward-model evidence; the forks' coverage of the hidden test is unknown.
\end{itemize}
