# quantum-compilation

Verifier-in-the-loop circuit compression for the [Google Quantum AI — Verifiable Quantum
Circuit Compilation](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/google-verifiable-quantum-circuit-compilation)
Kaggle competition ($150k, closes 2027-01-20).

Compress three fault-tolerant quantum subroutines — a 1/√x phasing circuit for fusion
plasma models, a Reed–Solomon syndrome decoder for Decoded Quantum Interferometry, and a
FeMoCo Hamiltonian block encoding. No quantum hardware, no training data, no GPU: every
candidate is checked exactly by [kickmix](https://github.com/quantumlib/Qualtran/tree/main/kickmix),
which simulates reversible-classical + diagonal-phase circuits of tens of millions of gates
in seconds. Paper: [arXiv:2610.23832](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23832).

This is the `ai4science` thesis with the two halves handed over pre-separated — an exact
verifier that cannot be gamed, and an unbounded generator space. The only question is how
well the search loop around it is built.

**The finding that matters more than the score is that the chemistry part has almost no

**What the plateau did, in the shape's own terms.** The part is **R=8, B=143, C=60** — its
author's manifest figure, and the shape of the arrays in the `submission.npz` we submitted —
against the baseline's **R=65, B=60, C=60**, which the shipped verifier declares as
`BASELINE_SHAPE` in `scoreinfra/reference.py`. So the plateau **cut the rank from 65 to 8 and
widened the basis from 60 to 143**: 3,976 → 1,220 LCU vectors for the ANGLE circuits to carry
(the verifier builds [`N + R·B`](src/quantum_compilation/cost.py) of them, which is each npz's
`angle_words` row count), Toffolis 93,237 → 16,266, width 730 → 430. What it spent is ensemble
capacity R·C, down 8.1× from 3,900 to 480 — and that shows as accuracy: the 20 mHa cap the
reference clears at 93.2% is the one this part sits on, at 99.999%. Low rank is what the
plateau found; the accuracy cap is what stops it going lower.

**Refitting the factorization is closed — it cannot pay.** `lambda_eff` is **93.86% a fixed
constant of the reference Hamiltonian**: writing `D = 2Λ − E_gap` out in full cancels the
negative eigenvalue mass and leaves `D = 2P + 2(λ_h2 − ¼ΣW²) − c0` with both leading terms
provably non-negative, so `D ≥ −c0 = 559.132473` of our 595.73. That puts
[`lambda_eff_floor`](src/quantum_compilation/cost.py#L269) at 46.786 against our 48.293 — the
whole factorization search is worth **3.12%**, or **+0.243 blend** against the **+1.82** that
25.0 needs, and the floor is unreachable at that. **DISCARDed before any compute was spent.**
[`vqcc chem`](src/quantum_compilation/cli.py#L175) prints the headroom on every run; the
derivation, its numerical verification and the literature sweep behind it are in
[`reports/2026-11-02-thc-factorization-sota.md`](reports/2026-11-02-thc-factorization-sota.md).

So the chemistry cost is **`T · Q` and essentially nothing else** — the ANGLE loaders' Toffoli
count and the 430-qubit footprint, not the fit.

**Half of `T` costs no accuracy, and the other half cannot be bought.** Driving the verifier's
own `SpinFreeData_V1` at our shape reproduces both recorded totals exactly:

| | Toffolis | share of `T` | bought with |
|---|---|---|---|
| ANGLE circuits | 8,336 | **51.25%** | **nothing — circuits a submission writes** |
| inner state prep | 5,311 | 32.65% | the shape `(R, B, C)` |
| Givens rotations | 2,250 | 13.83% | `brot` |
| outer state prep | 369 | 2.27% | the shape `(R, B, C)` |

and `Q = 430` as 304 shared workspace + 86 persistent state-prep qubits + 40 angle-word
registers (`J = 5` × `brot = 8`). Everything but the ANGLE row is closed-form in the shape and
`brot`, so it is paid for in error budget — and the error budget is spent, both residuals at
99.999% of the 20 mHa cap. The ANGLE Toffolis are the simulated mean of circuits the submitter
writes, so cheaper loaders for the *same* words score better at *identical* accuracy.

[`vqcc shape`](src/quantum_compilation/cli.py#L476) prices it: one Toffoli is **+0.000475
blend**, one qubit **+0.017998** (38× a Toffoli, `Q` being 38× smaller). Reaching 25.0 needs
**20.28% off the chemistry cost** — 3,298 Toffolis, 87 qubits, or any mix. From the free share
alone that is a **39.6% cheaper ANGLE family**.

Every way of buying the *bought* part is closed, all three measured rather than argued.
**`brot` cannot go down**: re-rounding the submitted angles onto the `brot = 7` grid breaches
the cap by **118×** on `error_1`, and since the angles already sit on the cap at `brot = 8`, no
fresh fit at half the grid spacing closes two orders of magnitude. **`brot` cannot go up**: at
`brot = 9` the inner preparation's QROAM jumps to a branch taking the workspace from 304 to 607
qubits, costing **−3.27 blend** despite 1,316 fewer Toffolis. **The shape cannot be re-split**:
at constant `R·B = 1,144` the submitted `(8, 143)` is the best of all 16 integer factor pairs.
So the ANGLE loaders are the only direction left that costs no accuracy — and no top submission
has moved them; they are reused byte-identical across the plateau.

## The metric

Score is the geometric mean of three cost ratios against a reference submission that scores
exactly 1.0. The reference costs below are **measured**, by running the shipped verifiers
over `sample_submission/` at the default salt:

| Task | Cost | Reference cost | Reference shape |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `invsqrt` | `Q × C`, with `C = Toffolis + T/4` | 2,076,774.23 | Q=260, C=7,987.59 |
| `dqi` | `R(ε₊) × Q² × T`, with `R(ε) = ln2 / −ln(½ + 2√ε)` | 2.4439463583673 × 10¹³ | Q=1,893, T=5,725,108.4, R=1.1913 |
| `dfthcsos` | `λ_eff × T × Q`, with `λ_eff = √(E_gap(2Λ − E_gap))` | 3,355,599,588.32 | Q=730, T=93,237, λ_eff=49.3014 |

[`cost.py`](src/quantum_compilation/cost.py) is transcribed from the **shipped verifiers**,
not the [Evaluation page](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/google-verifiable-quantum-circuit-compilation/overview/evaluation),
which states the formulas but not the constants. Three things it omits, each of which
changes the number:

1. **A rotation costs `0.56·log₂(1/ε) + 5.3` T gates** ([mixed fallback synthesis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.10064)),
   where `ε` is a *single circuit-wide* synthesis error — the largest that still fits the
   accuracy budget the simulated error leaves over — not a per-Clifford-level constant. The
   reference's 37 rotations land at `ε = 1.53 × 10⁻¹²`, so they cost 27.3 T each.
2. **`T` is the simulated mean per shot, not the static gate count.** Measurement-based
   uncomputation makes it data-dependent; the static count is only an upper bound. The dqi
   reference executes 5,725,108.4 of a possible 5,725,461.
3. **`ε₊` is a one-sided Clopper–Pearson upper bound** at `δ = 2⁻¹²⁸`, not the observed
   failure fraction. A decoder that fails **0 of 102,400** shots is still bounded at
   8.66 × 10⁻⁴ and pays **R = 1.1913** — a 19% floor nobody can optimise away.

Two consequences worth holding onto:

- **Equal weight.** A 1% cost cut in any task buys the same 0.33% of score.
- **`dqi` pays twice for width.** Its `Q²` term makes a qubit saved worth double.

## Run it

```bash
uv sync --extra dev
uv run pytest            # 132 tests
uv run vqcc --help
```

[`mise.toml`](mise.toml) carries every command below as a named task, so `mise tasks` lists
them and `mise run verify path/to/submission --seed 1` is the `uv run vqcc verify` line. It
needs `mise trust` once, derives `VQCC_VERIFIERS` from `VQCC_DATA` (default
`~/quantum-compilation-data`), and `mise run check-all` is the whole gate CI runs — the ruff
complexity check and the test suite.

`verify` is the one that matters — it is the dev set:

```bash
export VQCC_VERIFIERS=path/to/verifiers      # the extracted competition `verifiers/` tree
uv run vqcc verify path/to/submission/ --seed 1   # the real score, free and unlimited
uv run vqcc check path/to/submission/        # layout + the 128-qubit cap, instant
uv run vqcc chem path/to/dfthcsos/submission.npz  # every chemistry constraint but
                                             #   ANGLE_WIDTH, in 0.66 s, no circuits
uv run vqcc shape docs/experiments/57834335-parts.yaml --blend 23.18119 --sweep-brot
                                             # where T and Q go, and what each is worth
uv run vqcc resources path/to/circuits/      # static gate counts — a triage bound
uv run vqcc blend --invsqrt 100941 --dqi 4.0334e11 --dfthcsos 3.3780e8
uv run vqcc calibration                      # the reference costs in force, and their provenance
uv run vqcc diagram circuit.kmb --flame      # kickmix's own SVG flame chart
```

Regenerating the committed artefacts, after a submission and after any result update:

```bash
uv run --extra tracking python scripts/build_parts_record.py \
    <parts-dir> <rows.jsonl> <submission-root> \
    --submission-ref 57834335 --submitted-at 2026-10-31T09:27:57Z
uv run --extra tracking python scripts/build_artifact.py   # page + progress plot
```

That second command writes both [`docs/artifact/index.html`](docs/artifact/index.html) (then
republish it) and
[`experiments/progress/vqcc/progress.png`](experiments/progress/vqcc/progress.png), the PR's
visual aid, rendered by `autoresearch.render` from the same ledger. Record a new leaderboard
score as `score:`/`submission:` on that experiment's entry in
[`verdicts.yaml`](docs/experiments/verdicts.yaml) and both pick it up — it is the only figure
in either that cannot be derived locally.

**There is no train/dev split in this competition**, so the dev set is the *official verifier
run locally*. The leaderboard runs this same code and rations measurements to five a day;
running it locally is free, so every candidate goes through `verify` and only a winner costs
a submission — on the two readings taken so far, with the agreement reported in Status above.

**Pass `--seed` to compare candidates.** Each verifier derives its simulator seed from the
*candidate's own bytes* — `int(circuit_sha256, 16)`, or `sha256(circuit_sha256 + salt)` when
a salt is given — so two different circuits draw two different seeds whatever the salt is
set to, and a re-run of one circuit is bit-identical. Left alone that puts an unmatched draw
inside every comparison. All three verifiers accept `--seed`, which overrides the hash; the
salt only reproduces the published default derivation. Reference run times on 10 CPU cores:
invsqrt 5.7 min (200,540,928 shots), dqi 2.4 min, dfthcsos 1 min.

**How much that draw is worth is measured** — five seeds on one unchanged submission
([`57834335-seeds.yaml`](docs/experiments/57834335-seeds.yaml)), and the tasks split into
two regimes:

| | across 5 seeds | reading |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `dfthcsos` | **0 ppm** | identical every seed — deterministic |
| `invsqrt` | 0.84 ppm | effectively deterministic |
| `dqi` | **4,589 ppm** | one discrete step: 0 or 1 failed shots |
| blend | 1,527 ppm (0.0354) | carried entirely by `dqi` |

The mechanism is a step, not a distribution: four seeds dropped no shots and returned
identical numbers to every digit, while one dropped exactly one shot in 102,400, lifting the
Clopper–Pearson bound from 8.6606e-4 to 9.1042e-4 and the repetition factor from 1.19126 to
1.19673 — a 0.459% cost penalty. **So a chemistry change can be judged on a single seed with
no noise allowance at all, and a `dqi` gain below 0.035 blend points cannot be confirmed by
one submission** (gate G2). Our 23.18119 was a zero-failure draw; the identical file could
legitimately return 23.1458.

`check` is the cheap pre-filter: it audits the layout the Evaluation page specifies —
`invsqrt`, `dqi`, and a `dfthcsos/` folder of `angle_k_0 … angle_k_<n-1>` with no gaps plus
`submission.npz` — from filenames and the npz index alone, so a structurally broken 905 MB
submission is rejected before anything is parsed. It then checks the one rejection knowable
without simulating: the 128-qubit ANGLE cap.

## Three deliberate refusals

Each exists because a plausible default would silently mis-rank every candidate.

**No cost is computed without the verifier — invsqrt included.** The scored Toffoli count is
a simulated mean and the rotation price depends on the accuracy margin the simulation leaves
over, so neither is knowable from a static reading of a circuit. `resources` reports static
counts and calls them a *bound*; [`verify`](src/quantum_compilation/verify.py) produces
costs. An earlier draft priced invsqrt statically and would have ranked candidates on a
number the leaderboard does not use.

**Every formula is checked against the verifier that owns it.**
[`Verification.model_cost`](src/quantum_compilation/verify.py) re-derives each verifier's own score
from the fields that verifier reported, and `vqcc verify` prints the comparison per task —
agreeing, disagreeing and *not evaluated* as three distinct states, so a model that never ran
cannot read as one that agreed. The three real reference outputs are committed as
[test data](tests/data/) so the check is a permanent regression test, not a one-off. It is a
*validation* of the transcription, not a predictor: the inputs it needs — the per-shot
Toffoli mean, `rotation_eps`, `eps_plus` — are obtainable only by simulating, so it cannot
price a candidate that has not been run.

**A gate checks what it was given, not what it assumed.**
[`dfthcsos_rejections`](src/quantum_compilation/cost.py) takes only the ANGLE circuit widths,
because they are the one hard constraint knowable before the official verifier runs — the
accuracy terms, `E_gap` and `Λ` all come out of a verifier run. Accepting them as optional and
defaulting them to zero would have let the gate invent rejections, so it does not accept them
at all; [`dfthcsos_constraint_margins`](src/quantum_compilation/cost.py) checks all four
against the same constraint table once a report exists.

## What is not established

- **We have not compressed anything ourselves.** The 23.18119 is an assembly of other
  people's published circuits; everything we built is apparatus. No claim here is a claim
  about a circuit we designed.
- **How often `dqi` drops a shot is unmeasured.** Its size is measured — one failed shot,
  worth 0.0354 blend points — but 1 failure across 5 seeds leaves the *frequency* anywhere in
  [0.5%, 71.6%], so the expected penalty on any one submission is not known. Pinning it needs
  the ≥ 48 draws gate G2 asks for.
- **Two leaderboard readings, neither on a circuit we designed.** The loop agreed with the
  leaderboard on the reference and on this assembly. That does not prove it agrees on a
  candidate our own search produces.
- **Whether a cheaper ANGLE family exists is unmeasured.** `T` and `Q` are now priced, and
  the 8,336 free Toffolis are large enough to reach 25.0 on their own at a 39.6% cut. That
  says the budget is there, not that the circuit is: nothing here has looked inside the
  loaders, and 39.6% off a design the whole frontier reuses may well be out of reach.
- **Why 9 teams cleared 25 while half the field sits near 22.7 is unexplained**, and which
  task holds the margin that separates them from the plateau is the most valuable unknown in
  the competition.
- Which task to commit to is **open** — see the design doc. The recommendation is
  `dfthcsos`, on skill fit and on its ratio being the weakest of the three (9.93 against
  invsqrt 20.62 and dqi 60.80). The qualification is that its width is governed by the
  state-prep workspace rather than by the circuits a submission ships — the ANGLE circuits are
  40 of our 126 persistent qubits and none of the 304 temporary ones — so `Q` is effectively
  fixed and `T` is the whole of what is left.
