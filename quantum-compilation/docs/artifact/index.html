The consequence is structural: no task’s cost is computable from a static reading of a circuit, invsqrt included. The earlier static pricing path was removed rather than corrected — it would have ranked candidates on a number the leaderboard does not use.

The page uses “T gates” for the rotation price and “Toffolis” for the gate count the cost formulas call T. They are different quantities: invsqrt is scored on C = Toffolis + T gates/4.

The 19% nobody can optimise away

A decoder that fails zero of 102,400 shots is still bounded at ε₊ = 8.66e-04, so dqi pays R = 1.1913 — a 19% repetition overhead — before any circuit work. Only more shots lower that floor, and the shot count is the verifier’s to choose.

What a chemistry submission actually controls

The ANGLE circuits a submission ships are 85% of the Toffoli count but only about half the persistent width — and they add nothing at all to the temporary workspace, which is a maximum over families and is dominated by inner state prep. Width is governed by a parameterisation, not by the circuits a submission writes.

The reference sits close to three cliffs, all of which reject outright rather than gracefully: the widest ANGLE circuit at 119 qubits against the 128 cap (93% of it), error_2 at 0.01864 Ha against a 0.020 Ha cap (93%), and E_gap at 3.9655 Ha against a strict 3.914898 Ha floor (+1.3%). How far a candidate can move before one of them fires has not been measured.

Promotion gates

G0The cost model is calibrated: measuring the competition's own sample_submission/ scores 1.0000 to four decimal places, and the measured numbers are written into a calibration JSON with measured=true. Nothing downstream is trustworthy until this passes, and vqcc says so on every run until it does.
G1A local change beats the current submitted baseline by more than measured submission-to-submission noise, established from one identical re-run.
G2For dqi, the gain holds as a reduction in *expected* failures across >= 48 independent draws, not on the single draw seeded by the candidate file's own hash.
G3The improvement survives an external leaderboard submission, not only the local verifier at its default salt.

Not established

  • No compression has been attempted. Everything here is measurement apparatus.
  • Run-to-run noise is unmeasured. Every verifier seeds itself from the candidate’s own bytes — int(circuit_sha256, 16), or sha256(hash + salt) with a salt — so a re-run of one circuit is bit-identical and two candidates never share a draw. Comparing candidates needs an explicit --seed; sizing the across-seed noise is gate G1 and the next thing to run.
  • Every third-party number here is unverified by us. The per-task frontier costs and the 23.13587 blend are self-reported in Kaggle topic 754174, and the blend check above rests entirely on them — if they are wrong, that check says nothing. The leaderboard scores and the 1,756 team count are the competition’s own.
  • How much dfthcsos headroom exists is unmeasured. The reference sits at 93% of the ANGLE qubit cap and 93% of the error_2 budget, and nobody has measured how far a candidate can move before one rejects.
  • One leaderboard reading, on an input designed to score 1.0. Agreement there does not prove agreement on a compressed candidate.
  • Which task to commit to is open. The structural facts above bear on it; the decision does not follow from them.
  • Why 9 teams cleared 25 while half the field sits near 22.6 is unexplained, and which task holds that margin is the most valuable unknown in the competition.
