The consequence is structural: no task’s cost is computable from a static reading of a circuit, invsqrt included. The earlier static pricing path was removed rather than corrected — it would have ranked candidates on a number the leaderboard does not use.

The 19% nobody can optimise away

A decoder that fails zero of 102,400 shots is still bounded at ε₊ = 8.66e-04, so dqi pays R = 1.1913 — a 19% repetition overhead — before any circuit work. Only more shots lower that floor, and the shot count is the verifier’s to choose.

What a chemistry submission actually controls

The ANGLE circuits a submission ships are 85% of the Toffoli count but only about half the persistent width — and they add nothing at all to the temporary workspace, which is a maximum over families and is dominated by inner state prep. Width is governed by a parameterisation, not by the circuits a submission writes.

What one Toffoli is worth, and which ones are for sale

Driving the verifier’s own cost model at the plateau’s shape reproduces its recorded T and Q exactly, and splits them: 8,044 of the 14,706 Toffolis (54.7%) are ANGLE circuits, which a submission writes and so can make cheaper at identical accuracy. The rest is closed-form in (R, B, C) and brot, payable only in accuracy — and the accuracy is spent, the binding residual at 100.000% of its cap.

At this cost, one Toffoli is worth +0.000639 blend and one qubit +0.022005 — a qubit is worth 34 Toffolis purely because Q is that much smaller. Reaching the gold line, 28.824, from chemistry alone needs 6.47% off this cost: 952 Toffolis, or 28 qubits, or any mix. Out of the free share alone that is loaders 12% cheaper — the only direction left that costs no accuracy. What those circuits turn out to be is what changes that number’s meaning.

Every way of buying the bought half is closed, measured rather than argued. brot cannot go down: re-rounding the submitted angles onto the brot = 7 grid breaches the 20 mHa cap by 118× on error_1, and they already sit on that cap at 8, so a fresh fit at half the grid spacing would have to beat re-rounding by two orders of magnitude. brot cannot go up: at 9 the inner preparation’s QROAM jumps to a branch taking the shared workspace from 304 to 607 qubits, costing −3.27 blend despite 1,316 fewer Toffolis. And the shape cannot be re-split: at a constant R·B = 1,144 the submitted (8, 143) beats all 16 integer factor pairs.

The reference sits close to three cliffs, all of which reject outright rather than gracefully: the widest ANGLE circuit at 119 qubits against the 128 cap (93% of it), the binding accuracy term at 0.01864 Ha against a 0.020 Ha cap (93%), and E_gap at 3.9655 Ha against a strict 3.914898 Ha floor (+1.3%). Those are the reference’s margins, read off the same constraint table; the chart above measures the same three for the plateau part, which has spent nearly all of them.

The loaders are Boolean circuits, and the width cap pins their batch size

The free half is not a lookup table. Each ANGLE_k is a GF(2) circuit: a ladder of AND-ancillas, 33,540 CNOTs fanning their products into the word bits — 27,444 of them targeting the selection register itself, used in place as a linear scratchpad and restored by the end — then measurement and a conditioned CZ clearing every ancilla. The verifier charges Toffoli-like gates only, and a conditioned gate only on the shots whose bit is set, so XOR costs nothing and the AND gates are the whole bill. A loader’s price is the multiplicative complexity of its word table, not its address count — which is why 1,220 addresses cost 258 Toffolis and not 1,219.

Rebuilding the same angle_words three ways sizes the construction: kickmix’s own init_lookup costs 30,690 Toffolis per pass over the family, the starter kit’s segment-tree QROM 18,270, and the submitted family 4,022 — 7.6× cheaper than the library primitive and 4.5× cheaper than the starter kit, bought with 65 extra workspace qubits. It is nobody’s default.

The submitted mean of 268.1 ANDs per circuit is 1.708× that floor, and J — the batch size — is free: the contract fixes only that a family covers all 75 angles and that no circuit exceeds 128 qubits. The floor rises with J, since K falls as 1/J while a circuit’s floor grows only as √J. The construction does not. Simulated gate by gate, every loader keeps a pool of address functions in its workspace — selection bits plus an AND ladder — and writes each output bit as a sum of products of two pool combinations, about 1,220 / (2 × pool) per bit. That model, calibrated once (0.757×) to the measured family, prices every batching. The word registers eat the pool: J=13 leaves 13 workspace qubits, and the model puts it at 21.03, -7.16. The best batching is J=4 at +0.020, inside the model’s error, so re-batching is no lever and J=5 is where the width cap puts it.

This corrects an earlier revision of the page, which held the submitted multiple of the floor fixed across J and read +1.04 off J=13 — the floor ignores width, and that rate is unreachable once the pool is gone. Two more facts bound what is left. The circuits never read the word registers as inputs: zeroed, they still add exactly the right delta, so they work against 191, not 157, at 1.40×. And 28.8244 at J=5 needs 236 ANDs per circuit, below that 191: no address-only construction reaches the band. The floor’s 31.482 at J=11, and 31.51 with lambda_eff at its floor too, need a loader that reads the word registers and adds the same delta whether they hold the previous batch or this one, as the reverse pass requires. Whether one exists is not established.

Re-verifying one unchanged submission under 5 verifier seeds splits the three tasks into two regimes. r3-crack-the-angle-loadersKEEP

gate none -- the same reach calculation as r1 and r2, run before writing a single circuit · 2026-11-03 · PR #104

The submitted loaders are not lookup tables. Each ANGLE_k is a GF(2) circuit: a ladder of AND-ancillas, then ~33,500 CNOTs fanning their products into the word bits, then HMR plus conditioned CZ uncomputing every ancilla at Clifford cost. Of circuit 13's 33,540 CNOTs, 27,444 target the selection register itself, used in place as a linear scratchpad and restored by the end; 183 of its 258 Toffolis write straight into word bits and 75 build reusable ancillas. Since recover_words_from_circuits charges Toffoli-like gates only -- and "a conditioned gate counts only on the shots whose bit is set", so the measurement-based uncompute is free -- XOR costs nothing and the AND gates are the entire bill. A loader's price is the multiplicative complexity of its word table, not its address count. Rebuilding the same angle_words three ways sizes that construction: kickmix's own init_lookup costs 30,690 Toffolis per pass over the family (2,046 per circuit, 62 qubits), the starter kit's segment-tree QROM with Qualtran's _break_early costs 18,270 (1,218 per circuit, 61 qubits), and the submitted family costs 4,168 (258-301 per circuit, 126 qubits). So the plateau is already 7.4x cheaper than the library primitive and 4.4x cheaper than the starter kit, bought with 65 extra workspace qubits, and it is nobody's default -- it is a hand-built construction. How much is left is a counting question. A straight-line GF(2) circuit with n inputs, A AND gates and b outputs realises at most 2(2An + A2 + A + b*(n + A + 1)) distinct maps, while a family member must reproduce a specific b-bit word on each of 1,220 addresses -- one of 2**(b*1220) tables -- so all but a vanishing fraction need A**2 + A*(2n + 1 + b) + b*(n + 1) >= b*1220. n is not 11 but 51: each circuit runs with its word registers already holding the previous batch, itself a function of the address, so a floor must grant them as 40 nonlinear inputs. At b = 40 that gives A >= 157, against 191 for the address bits alone. The submitted circuits never use them: simulated gate by gate from a zeroed register, every circuit still adds exactly the right delta, so their 277.9 mean is 1.45x the address-only bound and 1.770x the granted one. Using them is harder than it looks: the reverse pass runs the same circuit from this batch back to the previous one, so a delta read off the registers must agree from both. The floor rises with the batch size J; the construction falls. Every loader keeps a pool of address functions in its workspace -- selection bits plus an AND ladder -- and writes each output bit as a sum of products of two pool combinations, about 1220 / (2 * pool) of them per bit. That model, calibrated once (0.784x) to the measured family, prices every batching. The word registers eat the pool -- J=13 leaves 13 workspace qubits -- so re-batching is no lever: best is J=4 at +0.024, inside the model's error, and J=13 is -5.69. Correction: an earlier revision held the 1.770x rate fixed across J and read +1.041 at J=13 off it; the floor ignores width, and that rate is unreachable once the pool is gone. So the floor's escape band is a statement about word-reading constructions only. It permits 25.0 at every J >= 5 (best J=11 at 25.684; 25.957 with lambda_eff at its own floor too), but at J=5, 25.0 needs 168 ANDs per circuit, below the address-only bound of 191 -- no construction of the kind anyone has submitted reaches the band through the loaders. Each 20 ANDs per circuit saved at J=5 is worth about +0.3. Not established: that a loader can read its word registers consistently in both directions, or that the pool model -- fitted at J=5 and extrapolated -- holds for constructions other than the submitted one.
